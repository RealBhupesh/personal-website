A shopkeeper in Nashik receives a payment. A truck crosses a toll plaza. A farmer records what she has sown. Each action leaves a small digital trace.

Taken alone, a trace answers a narrow question. Taken carefully together, the traces could tell a larger story.

India has built much of the machinery that records these events. The opportunity is to make that machinery useful for understanding the economy, without making every citizen more visible to the state.

That distinction is the heart of this essay. A country should be able to notice a failing harvest or a disappearing market without needing to watch every person inside it.

## A fast economy, measured slowly

In February 2026, India [rebased its GDP series from 2011-12 to 2022-23](https://www.pib.gov.in/PressReleasePage.aspx?PRID=2233518&lang=2&reg=48). The new methods draw on sources including GST, vehicle registrations, and the Public Financial Management System.

That is a meaningful improvement. It also exposes a persistent gap: economic activity happens continuously, while a reliable description of it takes time to assemble.

A payment can settle in seconds. GDP cannot. **GDP measures value added, not the amount of money that changes hands.** A transfer between two accounts is not necessarily new production. Several payments may belong to the same supply chain.

A larger nominal receipt may reflect a higher price rather than more goods sold. A digital payment may replace cash without changing total spending. Treating every extra transaction as extra output would produce a very confident mistake.

The useful ambition is therefore not live GDP on a glowing dashboard. It is a faster, well-calibrated view of particular activities, accompanied by an honest account of what remains unseen.

## The wrong race, and the useful one

India should build language models, scientific models, and the people who know how to use them. But leadership on a general-purpose benchmark is not the only way to create an important AI capability.

The [IndiaAI Mission has an approved five-year outlay of ₹10,371.92 crore](https://www.pib.gov.in/PressReleasePage.aspx?PRID=2245233&lang=1&reg=3), spread across compute, models, datasets, applications, skills, financing, and safety. Those are different investments.

A language model learns patterns from its training material. It can explain an invoice, extract a clause, or translate a question.

It does not thereby know whether a particular consignment moved yesterday or a particular district lost work this week.

For that, a system needs authorised access to current evidence, consistent definitions, and a way to test whether its interpretation is right. Model weights cannot substitute for those things.

My argument is about this surrounding system. India's distinctive opportunity lies in turning its digital public infrastructure into dependable instruments for understanding and action.

## What the six sources can actually see

Administrative data is a record of an interaction with a system. It is not a complete record of economic life. Each source illuminates something different, and each casts its own shadow.

### Payments: a receipt is a signal, not a census

UPI records payment events. Depending on the participant and authorised dataset, those events can supply timing, amount, counterparty, and merchant-related information. An app, a bank, and NPCI do not necessarily hold identical fields.

Location and purchase category should not be assumed to be complete or accurate. A merchant's registered address is not proof of where a purchase happened. A payment reference is not an itemised shopping basket.

Cash, adoption changes, refunds, and transfers between a person's own accounts complicate the picture. Payments can help measure activity, but they cannot stand in for all consumption.

### Goods: an intention to move is not proof of delivery

An [e-way bill documents a consignment's movement](https://docs.ewaybillgst.gov.in/html/faq.html), generally above ₹50,000, subject to exceptions. It can connect goods descriptions, origin, destination, and transport details.

A bill can be cancelled, updated, duplicated in an ingestion pipeline, or recorded before movement occurs. It is a useful piece of evidence. It is not a sensor attached to the goods.

### Roads: a crossing is a partial observation

[FASTag uses RFID for toll payments](https://www.npci.org.in/product/netc/about-netc). A toll event can help establish that a tagged vehicle passed a particular collection point at a particular time.

It does not reveal every road travelled, whether a truck was full, or which invoice its load belonged to. A route without a relevant toll crossing produces no such observation. Absence from the feed is not absence from the road.

### Finance: consent is a route, not a reservoir

The [Account Aggregator framework](https://rbikehtahai.rbi.org.in/AccountAggregatorFacility.html) enables a person to authorise financial information sharing. RBI explains that an AA does not see or store the customer's financial information.

The source institution and authorised recipient still matter. Consent must specify a purpose and scope. An application for one loan must not quietly become permission to build a permanent, cross-sector profile.

### Farms: a record is not the whole harvest

[AgriStack includes farmer, village-map, and crop-sown registries](https://www.pib.gov.in/PressReleasePage.aspx?PRID=2296209&lang=1&reg=3). These are foundations for linking agricultural services to people, places, and crops.

Knowing what was recorded as sown does not prove what was harvested. Land records may miss tenants or shared cultivation. Cloud cover, mixed plots, and weak ground measurements can make remote estimates uncertain.

### Work: keep the survey in the picture

The Periodic Labour Force Survey is different. It asks people about work; it is not transactional exhaust. Its [redesigned sampling supports monthly rural and urban estimates](https://static.pib.gov.in/WriteReadData/specificdocs/documents/2025/aug/doc2025818615201.pdf).

That difference is a strength. Surveys can observe work that never produces a payroll entry or a merchant payment. Their sampling and uncertainty need to be respected, rather than overwritten by a more frequent but narrower feed.

## The missing layer is a set of permissions

It is tempting to draw all six sources flowing into one enormous data lake. Technically, that is the easiest diagram. Institutionally, it may be the worst design.

A weekly statistical release, a loan application, and a fraud investigation are three different purposes. They need different records, access rules, safeguards, and people who can be held accountable.

I would design three separate paths. They could share standards and security practices without sharing a universal view of every person.

<Figure src="/writing/country-that-can-see-itself/three-permissions.webp" alt="Three separate illustrated channels with individual gates: aggregate observations reach a public statistical instrument, a person's authorised records reach a lender, and specific evidence reaches a case reviewer. The channels do not merge." width="1536" height="1024" fullSizeLink="true" caption="1. Public statistics. 2. Consented services. 3. Lawful investigations. Each purpose has its own gate; access does not pass from one channel to another." />

**For public statistics, publish protected aggregates.** A statistical body might receive district-and-week totals, with disclosure controls applied before release. Most readers need an index, not transaction-level access.

**For a personal service, authorise a bounded exchange.** A borrower might permit a named lender to fetch a specified period of financial records for a particular application. Access should expire; later reuse needs its own justification.

**For enforcement, require the appropriate legal process.** A suspicious pattern is not a licence for an analyst, or a model, to explore a citizen's unrelated life. Case access and statistical access should be separated and logged.

The difficult engineering starts even before modelling. A business name may change. A payment handle may not identify a shop uniquely. District boundaries move. An invoice timestamp and a settlement timestamp refer to different events.

Where a lawful common identifier exists, it can support an exact join. Elsewhere, matching may be uncertain. The system must preserve that uncertainty instead of turning a probable match into a fact about someone.

Replacing an identifier with a hash does not make a joined record anonymous. A stable token still links events. Rare combinations of place, time, and activity can identify people even when their names have gone.

Small-cell suppression helps, but repeated queries can reveal what a single release hides. [NIST's privacy guidance](https://www.nist.gov/blogs/cybersecurity-insights/differential-privacy-privacy-preserving-data-analysis-introduction-our) explains why removing identifiers and aggregating records are not complete guarantees.

Differential privacy offers a more explicit approach: bound how much the probability of any published result can change when one person's records are added or removed.

The protection depends on bounded contributions, calibrated noise, and a privacy budget across repeated releases.

It is not a magic filter that makes every dataset safe. Someone still has to decide which questions are permitted, what accuracy is lost, and whether a release can harm a small community even without identifying its members.

## From a stream of events to a weekly pulse

Consider the first product I would build: a weekly indicator of local activity. Not a district GDP estimate, and not an automated verdict on whether a place is prospering.

**Nowcasting** means estimating what is happening now when the definitive measurement has not yet arrived.

Work by [Galbraith and Tkacz on electronic payments](https://www.ecb.europa.eu/pub/pdf/scpsps/ecbsp10.en.pdf) shows how payment information can improve short-term GDP estimates in a studied setting.

That is a reason to investigate an Indian system, not evidence that the same performance will appear in every district. The target, coverage, and publication frequency would have to be chosen and validated locally.

<Figure src="/writing/country-that-can-see-itself/from-signal-to-estimate.webp" alt="An artist's sequence of measurement instruments: diverse observations enter a checking station, a calendar and comparison mechanism align them, and a final gauge reports an estimate with a visible range of uncertainty." width="1536" height="1024" fullSizeLink="true" caption="1. Observe. 2. Reconcile. 3. Estimate. The final instrument reports a claim with uncertainty, rather than a direct reading of the economy." />

First, preserve the events as they were received. Keep event time separate from arrival time, record the source and schema version, and make retries idempotent: processing the same event twice must not count it twice.

Then build explicitly defined weekly measures. Decide how reversals, cancelled bills, late records, holidays, price changes, and shifting merchant coverage are handled. Document these choices as part of the statistic.

Next, compare the measures with trusted benchmarks. A simple seasonal baseline should be the first competitor. A state-space model or another forecasting method earns its place only if it improves decisions and error estimates.

**Time is part of the test.** To simulate a forecast made last June, use only information available last June. Training on later revisions and scoring against them can make a model look useful in a world it never actually faced.

The release should show coverage, missing feeds, revisions, and uncertainty. A district with too few observations should receive a coarser estimate, or no estimate, rather than a precise-looking number borrowed from its neighbours.

### When the pipes change, the signal changes

The Bank of England suspended its weekly card-spending series in December 2024 after an unexpected fall. Its investigation found that fewer merchant-acquirer payments were travelling through CHAPS. The [series was discontinued in April 2025](https://www.bankofengland.co.uk/payments/chaps-faster-indicator).

The measurement moved because the payment plumbing changed. Reading the fall as a sudden collapse in spending would have told the wrong economic story.

An Indian pulse would need the same humility. An outage, a new routing arrangement, or a shift from cash to digital payments can change a feed while the underlying activity changes much less.

I would track feed health beside the estimate, test for structural breaks, and retain a manual release decision. The right response to a broken instrument is sometimes to stop publishing, not to add another decimal place.

## Ten things India could build

The weekly pulse is the first of ten possibilities. The remaining nine need different models and different evidence. A language model, a graph model, and an econometric model are not interchangeable names for the same machine.

The following are proposals. Existing infrastructure makes parts of them plausible; it does not mean the whole system has been built, approved, or shown to work.

### 1. A weekly economic pulse

Start with a small set of protected regional indicators, published with coverage and revision notes. Separate the questions: spending, goods movement, and employment each deserve their own measure before anyone combines them.

Judge success by whether the release adds reliable information beyond existing statistics. More frequent publication is useful only if it tells us something we can trust.

### 2. Credit for the enterprise without a balance sheet

A kirana owner may have a useful record of receipts without a polished set of accounts. The problem is real: a [SIDBI-commissioned 2025 study estimated an addressable MSME credit gap of about ₹30 lakh crore](https://www.sidbi.in/uploads/Understanding_Indian_MSME_sector_Progress_and_Challenges_13_05_25_Final.pdf).

With appropriate authorisation, a lender could build cash-flow features: regularity of receipts, seasonality, reversals, concentration in a few customers, and the gap between inflows and obligations.

A credit model then needs outcomes, not just payments. Training on loans requires careful labels for repayment, default, restructuring, and the period over which each outcome was observed.

There is a selection problem: rejected applicants rarely come with the same repayment history as approved borrowers. A model trained only on yesterday's approvals can reproduce yesterday's exclusions.

[RBIH's Unified Lending Interface](https://rbihub.in/projects/unified-lending-interface) helps connect lenders with data and service providers. It is infrastructure for an authorised workflow, not itself a public credit score or a promise that a loan will be approved.

I would make reusable feature definitions and evaluation methods available, while keeping lenders responsible for their decisions. A borrower should receive a specific reason and a route to correct an incorrect record.

### 3. An invoice-graph detective

A business relationship has several traces: an invoice, a goods movement, perhaps a toll observation, and a settlement. A graph represents these as entities and time-stamped relationships rather than isolated rows in a spreadsheet.

A model could look for unusual chains, improbable timing, repeated patterns of circular invoicing, or inconsistencies between declared movement and independent observations. It could help investigators decide where to look first.

<Figure src="/writing/country-that-can-see-itself/following-the-evidence.webp" alt="An illustrated evidence graph connects a seller and a buyer through three distinct routes: an invoice ledger, a goods vehicle, and a bank settlement. A magnifying instrument examines a gap rather than stamping either person guilty." width="1536" height="1024" fullSizeLink="true" caption="1. Invoice. 2. Goods movement. 3. Settlement, flowing back from buyer to seller. A missing connection is a question for review, not proof of fraud." />

But a missing edge is not a conviction. Payment may be on credit. Goods may travel on an untolled road. One settlement may pay several invoices. An exemption or delayed record can explain an apparent mismatch.

The system should return an evidence bundle: the records used, the uncertain matches, the time window, and plausible alternative explanations. Human review must be able to reject the model's suggestion.

Precision matters more than a dramatic detection count. A system that finds more suspicious businesses by accusing many legitimate ones has not necessarily improved tax administration.

### 4. A national scam shield

[RBIH's MuleHunter](https://docs.rbihub.in/mule-hunter) uses supervised machine learning to identify likely mule accounts. Its documentation describes deployment inside a bank's own infrastructure, with stages progressing toward near-real-time use.

That is an important starting point. A cross-bank extension would be a further undertaking, with its own legal authority, data-sharing limits, latency budget, and operational responsibilities.

A fast money trail can cross institutions before any one bank sees the whole pattern. Useful cooperation might exchange tightly scoped risk signals rather than copying everyone's transaction history into one central store.

Speed alone is not the objective. Warnings must reach the right person, in a language they understand, while legitimate payments remain usable. Measure false holds, correction time, and loss prevented together.

### 5. From monsoon to money

The agriculture ministry describes [YES-TECH as a technology-based yield-estimation initiative](https://www.pib.gov.in/PressReleasePage.aspx?PRID=2296209&lang=1&reg=3). Satellite observations can contribute to that estimate, alongside weather, crop information, and ground measurements.

A satellite measures reflected or emitted signals. A model relates those observations to vegetation and yield using examples. It does not look down and directly read the number of bags a farmer will harvest.

An area-level estimate can also miss damage on one particular plot. This is **basis risk**: the measurement used by a scheme and the loss experienced by the person do not line up.

<Figure src="/writing/country-that-can-see-itself/a-right-to-question.webp" alt="A circular illustrated process connects a satellite observing crops, an estimated result, a farmer submitting a ground photograph, and a human reviewer returning a corrected decision and feedback to the model." width="1536" height="1024" fullSizeLink="true" caption="1. Observe. 2. Estimate. 3. Submit field evidence. 4. Human review and feedback. A farmer must be able to bring the field back into the model." />

A usable appeal should accept local evidence and offer assistance when the person has no smartphone. Reviewers need the original observation dates, the model version, its uncertainty, and a way to record why the estimate was challenged.

The next ambition could be earlier financial relief after a verified crop shock. A loan-rescheduling proposal should be tested separately from the yield model; a good estimate does not automatically make a good lending policy.

### 6. A financial guardian in every language

A public assistant could help a person understand a policy, a loan offer, or a fee schedule before signing. Its job would be to make a document inspectable, not to push the person toward a product.

The technical pattern is retrieval-augmented generation: fetch the relevant, versioned document passages, then ask a language model to explain them with citations. Use deterministic calculations for fees and repayment amounts.

A citation is useful only if the passage supports the answer. Test that relationship directly, including exclusions, ambiguous clauses, and cases where the safest useful answer is that the document does not settle the question.

Speech adds another failure point. A name, an amount, or a decimal can be misheard. The assistant should read consequential details back for confirmation, and have a route to qualified human help.

### 7. A flight simulator for policy

A forecast asks what may happen next. A policy simulator asks what would change if someone intervened. Those are different questions.

If spending and a tax change move together, a predictive model can learn the association without learning the effect of the tax. Seasonality, anticipation, and other policy changes may explain part of what it sees.

A serious simulator needs an explicit causal question, defensible assumptions, and evidence for its counterfactual: what would have happened without the intervention. It should compare scenarios and expose sensitivity to those assumptions.

This remains the most speculative proposal here. The output should be a range of conditional possibilities, not a beautifully animated forecast presented as authority.

### 8. A jobs radar for the informal economy

Merchant activity and provident-fund enrolments could add context to labour statistics. They cannot, by themselves, tell us who has decent work, how many hours someone worked, or what that work paid.

An enrolment can reflect formalisation rather than a new job. Growing receipts can come from higher prices or longer hours for the same workers. A closing shop may move online rather than disappear.

I would use these sources as supplementary indicators, tested against PLFS and local surveys. Start at a geography where the benchmark is defensible.

District-level estimates should not be promised just because a payment feed has district labels.

### 9. A sandbox and benchmark for Indian finance AI

A useful sandbox would contain carefully governed test cases and synthetic datasets that resemble Indian financial workflows. It should let builders fail safely before they interact with someone's money or eligibility.

Synthetic data is not automatically anonymous. A generator can reproduce details from its source material. Release testing should look for memorisation and leakage, alongside whether the data covers useful and difficult situations.

The benchmark should test Indian languages, mixed-language speech, missing records, unusual repayment schedules, and changing document versions.

Include tasks with no answer. Measure calibrated uncertainty and citation accuracy as well as task success.

Publish the evaluation method, rotate hidden test cases, and hold out institutions and later time periods. Otherwise, a system can become excellent at recognising the exam and remain poor at the work.

### 10. Export the architecture, with local control

The exportable product is more than a payment interface or a pretrained model. It is a set of standards, bounded data exchanges, evaluation tools, and operating practices that another country can inspect and adapt.

A model trained on Indian merchant activity may perform badly where cash use, crops, language, or banking access differ. Importing software is not the same as importing its calibration.

Partners should retain control over their data, permissions, and appeals. Shared infrastructure is valuable when it increases local capability, rather than requiring every difficult question to be sent back to its exporter.

## The safeguards are part of the architecture

The most dangerous diagram is the one with many inputs and no way back from the output. It makes people into records and decisions into destinations.

A responsible system needs a return path. A person can inspect a relevant record, challenge an incorrect match, submit missing evidence, and learn what changed. That path must work for someone with a basic phone and limited time.

There is also a collective risk. If every lender uses the same model, one mistaken assumption can become a market-wide pattern.

Public models should support independent evaluation and alternatives, not become an unquestionable national score.

The statistical system needs an equally strong boundary. Publishing aggregate trends should not quietly build a shortcut into enforcement or individual eligibility decisions.

Separate access, separate retention, and independent scrutiny are design choices.

I would require a release record for every consequential output: the source versions, missing data, permissions used, model version, evaluation results, responsible institution, and route to correction.

Some of that record belongs with an authorised reviewer, some with the affected person, and some in public documentation. Transparency does not mean publishing the private data that made a decision possible.

## A proposed sequence for 2027 and 2028

The dates below are a suggested programme, not an announced government plan. Each stage should depend on evidence from the one before it.

**First, build the measurement contract.** Define a small public-interest question, the permitted data, a disclosure policy, independent oversight, and what would cause the project to stop. Publish the methodology before publishing the index.

A proposed Economic Data Trust could coordinate this work. Its powers, exclusions, funding, and independence would need to be established explicitly. A new name alone creates neither lawful access nor public trust.

**Then, run a narrow pilot.** Choose a few willing regions and institutions. Run the indicator in parallel with existing measures. Start with delayed, coarse releases and expand only after testing coverage, privacy, revisions, and usefulness.

In parallel, test service-specific systems in controlled settings. A lending workflow, a fraud-review tool, and an insurance appeal need separate evaluations. They should not inherit a seal of approval from the statistical pilot.

**Finally, open the useful parts.** Publish reproducible aggregate methods, appropriate synthetic data, benchmark definitions, and independent evaluations. Expand geography or frequency only where the evidence supports it.

The milestones should be outcomes: fewer unsupported decisions, faster corrections, useful early signals, and a record the public can scrutinise. A rising count of models is not an adequate substitute.

## The country that can see itself

India's digital infrastructure has made many ordinary actions easier to record. Whether that becomes an advantage in AI depends on what we choose to ask of the record, and what we refuse to let it become.

The prize is a government that notices a local downturn sooner, a lender that can understand an enterprise without demanding the wrong paperwork, and a farmer who can question an estimate without first becoming a technical expert.

It is a more demanding ambition than joining databases and calling the result intelligence. It asks for good instruments, limited powers, published methods, and institutions capable of admitting that an instrument is wrong.

A useful mirror should help a country understand itself. It should not require its people to become transparent.
